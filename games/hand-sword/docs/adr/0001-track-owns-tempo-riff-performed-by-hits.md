# Track owns tempo and difficulty; Riffed Tracks are performed by player hits, not autoplayed

Tempo and Difficulty are both intrinsic to each Theme, not independently player-chosen — a Riffed Track (real-song-inspired cover) must run at close to its real-world BPM to stay recognizable, and a track's difficulty is already implied by its tempo and arrangement, so a separate difficulty control on top of the Theme picker was redundant (and could put a player in an inconsistent combination, e.g. "hard" density over a track that's musically easy). Difficulty (`easy`/`medium`/`hard`, fixed per Theme) controls Box spawn density only. For Riffed Tracks specifically, only the notes sampled onto spawned Boxes are heard at full volume when the player hits them; other notes in the riff play quietly as backing texture — the melody is deliberately performed by the player's hits rather than autoplaying the source track underneath the game.

This is hard to reverse without breaking why the cover Themes work at all (recognizable tempo, riff tied to player input, density matched to the song's actual feel), and isn't obvious from the code alone — a reader could easily assume Difficulty is a separate player setting, that it controls speed, or that the riff plays automatically in the background.
